# Coming-soon scene options

Concepts for discussion, 23 October 2026. Two options for each remaining feature family; these are not implemented scenes. Every option needs a visible “Coming soon” label. Keep the observational format, shared stage width and fixed device scale from MOTION.md. The companion has no visitor-facing name.

## Evidence and scope

- [Automation ideas #60](https://github.com/getcolex/primer-v2/issues/60), including its comments: connected sheets, source changes, visible event → condition → action rules, and suggestions that run only after acceptance. Updated sites are proposed for publication; they do not publish invisibly.
- [Personal software #61](https://github.com/getcolex/primer-v2/issues/61): gardens supply material, guidance supplies intent, artifacts supply the interface. Lead with a concrete job rather than the abstract phrase “personal software.”
- [HTML pages #104](https://github.com/getcolex/primer-v2/issues/104): self-contained pages, tools, forms and diagrams live as garden things. The issue explicitly says the HTML track was not built on main as of 21 October.
- PDF news and video explainers are the owner's explicit desired integration examples. The issues support the broader output/implements direction; they do not establish that a particular PDF or video service is connected today.

## 1. Simple content automations

### A — The menu catches up (recommended)

Setting: extend Kitchen into a small café garden. A connected sheet and a menu-page preview sit beside a short rule.

1. The café owner accepts “When a price changes in this sheet, prepare an updated menu.” The accepted rule stays visible.
2. A later change updates “Flat white” from £3.50 to £3.80 in one sheet cell.
3. The same price changes in the menu preview. A small result says “Menu update ready” and “Review & publish.” Stop before publication.

Why it earns a scene: one obvious change travels from a connected data source into a useful output. It demonstrates ongoing behaviour, not another round of collecting. The publication step remains the owner's choice.

### B — The right mention reaches you

Setting: a small client/research garden, using the existing Small shop, less admin area.

1. A person accepts a plain-language rule: “When a new item mentions Acme, email me a short summary.”
2. Two website items arrive; only one contains the highlighted name. The matching item follows the rule; the unrelated item stays in the garden.
3. A compact email preview appears with the matching item's gist and source link. The visible run says “1 matching item · email sent.” Sending occurs only after the shown rule acceptance.

Why it earns a scene: event, condition and action are all visible. It shows a connected action beyond rearranging garden cards. Based on #60's client-mention integration example; all displayed people/content are illustrative.

## 2. Make small HTML pages and tools

### A — Reading becomes a game (recommended)

Setting: Fractions, by hand. Ms. Rao has notes on halves and quarters.

1. She asks “Make a pizza game for practising quarters.”
2. A small HTML-page card forms beside the source notes; it opens within the scene into the existing pizza visual.
3. Her scripted cursor selects two of eight slices. The page responds “2/8 = ¼.” It settles as a saved game in the garden.

Why it earns a scene: the output behaves, so it visibly differs from a generated summary. Reuse the pizza graphics/behaviour already on the page. In the scene, the actor plays; no additional visitor interaction is required.

### B — A question becomes a simulation

Setting: Money basics. A parent has saved an allowance note and a compounding explainer.

1. They ask “Make a little tool to compare saving £5 or £10 a week.”
2. A small page appears with a weekly-saving slider and two simple labelled curves.
3. The scripted human moves the slider; the projected totals and curve update together. The saved page remains beside its source material.

Why it earns a scene: it demonstrates an arbitrary tool generated from collected context. Any numbers must be labelled illustrative assumptions, not forecasts. Avoid financial advice or unnecessary realism in the mock calculation.

## 3. Custom AI actions in the sidebar

### A — My Sunday paper (recommended)

Setting: a reading/news garden. Three chosen articles, a sidebar and a document preview.

1. The person names a new sidebar action “My Sunday paper”: “Make a short PDF from the news I saved.” A small “Document tool connected” cue establishes that a connected service creates the output.
2. They run the action. The article titles become an orderly front page, with concise sections and source links.
3. A PDF card lands in the garden, ready to open. The action remains in the sidebar for next time.

Why it earns a scene: the lasting result is both a document and a reusable personal action. Show manual invocation here so it does not repeat the automation scene.

### B — Explain this to the lab

Setting: Lena's robotics material, presented as a separate coming-soon area so the launch scene stays unambiguous.

1. Lena creates a sidebar action “Explain this to the lab” and chooses her wheel-drift notes/references.
2. She runs it through a visibly connected video tool. Three storyboard frames form: drifting path, uneven wheel calibration, corrected path.
3. They resolve into a short draft video card with a poster and duration. A brief scripted preview shows the concept; the sources remain attached.

Why it earns a scene: a named action converts existing garden context into a different medium. Do not imply a named vendor is already integrated or that Grdn's native feature is a full video editor.

## Suggested first choices

Menu catches up + Reading becomes a game + My Sunday paper.

They demonstrate three different verbs: an accepted rule updates, a created tool responds, a reusable action produces a document. These are options for the owner to select, not authorization to build all six.
